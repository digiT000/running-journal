## 🤖 Coach Reflection

August was a month of two clear chapters. The first nine days converted July's endurance base into a genuine competitive result — a clean comeback from prior foot/leg discomfort, a new longest run, and then three PBs at the first official 10K event, capped by disciplined easy running the day after.

The second half was more instructive than smooth. Jumping straight from a 12.31 KM longest run to a 16 KM target didn't work on the first two tries — both cut short by heat, a fair and unavoidable environmental limiter. The third attempt showed real progress (12.04 KM) but introduced a new, more concerning limiter: foot pain. To the runner's credit, that pain didn't get pushed through — the following sessions backed off to easy and duration-based efforts, and by month-end the same volume was being rebuilt (three ~10.5–11 KM sessions at controlled HR) without any recurrence.

That adaptation — noticing a repeated failure pattern, changing the approach rather than repeating it, and listening to the body when pain showed up — is exactly the kind of decision-making that keeps a Half Marathon build on track. No sign of overtraining or unmanaged injury risk in the logged data.

**Recommendation for September:** attempt the next long run at a moderate step up (13–14 KM, not 16 KM) in cooler hours, resume weight logging, and keep "easy" days genuinely easy by watching HR rather than pace alone.
